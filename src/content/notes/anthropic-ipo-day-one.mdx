## Summary

I applied for A$10,000 of Anthropic's IPO, was allocated 11 shares at US$83 and sold them on the first trading day at US$97.61. That's 17.6% a share, or 16.2% (A$222) after commission and currency. This was a short-term alpha trade to lift portfolio returns. It was never a long-term position: the portfolio already carries heavy AI exposure through TSMC.

## Why not hold it

I already own TSMC, which makes the chips Anthropic's compute partners run on. Adding Anthropic long term would stack a second, larger bet on the same AI build-out. In a portfolio this concentrated, that's too much of one theme.

## Why the day-one pop

Large, high-profile IPOs are usually priced to leave something on the table. The question was whether that still held for a deal this size.

What this tells me:

- **The allocation is where the edge is.** Every one of these closed day one above its IPO price. The median gain was 19.2%.
- **Buying at the open is not.** Four of seven closed below their opening price. Anthropic gave back 12.9% from its first trade.
- **Hot deals fade fast intraday.** Anthropic opened at US$111.50 just before noon, hit US$115.10 within minutes, then fell to around US$95 within half an hour. It spent the afternoon between about US$93 and US$98. Even its low of US$92.72 was 11.7% above the IPO price.

- **This deal was priced to pop.** Anthropic set its range at US$70–78, about 20% below the valuation talk, then priced above it at US$83. Only about 6% of the company was offered, and index funds were due to buy within 5 to 15 days.

SpaceX and SK hynix rallied when options trading opened and index buying began. Cerebras and Arm gave back much of their pop. This week, all three of this year's biggest IPOs before Anthropic traded below their first trade.

I chose one day because AI stocks are unusually volatile right now. Nvidia's CEO left on 12 Nov in a scandal tied to chip sales into China, and chip stocks swung hard: AMD rose about 6% that day, then fell almost 9% the next. On Anthropic's debut, the Nasdaq fell 2.25%. Holding for index and options flows was a bet that the market would stay calm for a week. I didn't want that risk for a few extra points.

## Fundamentals at a glance

Not a full valuation, since this was a short-term trade. My read of the S-1:

- **Revenue is growing exponentially.** US$4.7bn in Q1, US$11.6bn in Q2 and US$17.0bn in Q3 2026, up 1,127% on Q3 2025.
- **Most of the loss is accounting.** Of the US$104bn nine-month net loss, US$94bn (91%) is the change in fair value of convertible notes and similar instruments, which rises with the company's valuation. The nine-month operating loss was US$1.3bn.
- **Cash flow and balance sheet are solid.** Operating cash flow was +US$6.6bn over nine months. Liquidity was US$146.6bn at 30 Sep.
- **Customers keep expanding.** Net revenue retention (NRR) is about 900%, meaning existing customers spend roughly nine times more than a year ago. Customers spending US$100,000+ a year rose from about 950 to more than 11,900.
- **The product is good.** I use Claude every day, including to build this site. That's a personal view, not data.

## What worried me

- **US$900bn of compute commitments.** Purchase and lease commitments total about US$900bn, mostly for compute and data centres, running to 2045. Around US$70bn falls due in the next 12 months.
- **Revenue isn't locked in.** About 80% of revenue is usage-based, and customers can cut usage with little notice. If demand slows, the commitments stay.
- **Margins slipped in Q3.** Compute costs grew 77% quarter on quarter against 47% revenue growth, and the company swung back to a small operating loss.
- **An AI downturn.** If data-centre spending pauses, a company that has pre-committed this much is exposed.

- **DCF base case:** revenue reaches about US$355bn by 2030, with a 28% operating margin from 2031. The WACC (weighted average cost of capital) is 11.1%.
- **Relative valuation:** I applied 15x annualised Q3 revenue, the median for listed AI companies. At US$83, Anthropic was priced at 22x.
- **Blended:** about US$64 a share, about 34% below where I sold.

The range is wide. A 1% change in WACC moves the base case between US$57 and US$77. On my numbers, the day-one price ran ahead of the value. That's one more reason I sold on day one instead of holding.
